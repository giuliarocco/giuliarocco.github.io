<h3 style="color:#0076df">Postdoctoral</h3>

**Combining MRI and optical spectroscopy to map microvascular function and oxygen metabolism in healthy and diseased brain**. Energetic dysfunction accompanies the most common brain diseases, including dementia, stroke and neuroinflammatory conditions such as Multiple Sclerosis (MS), for which the altered tissue physiology is suggested to be linked to the brain’s inflammatory status, tissue dysfunction and ultimately, tissue damage. Brain tissue energy supply and consumption have strong potential to be clinically informative in characterising disease states and guiding the development of new treatments. Calibrated fMRI approaches, based on combining blood oxygenation level dependent (BOLD) and arterial spin labelling (ASL) cerebral blood flow (CBF) measurements, can quantitatively map cerebrovascular function and the cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen. Our overall aim is to develop and validate minimally invasive MRI and demonstrate the potential of non-invasive optical imaging tools for mapping oxygen metabolism and cerebral microvascular function in the healthy and inflamed brain. Finally, a clinical proof-of-concept study will be performed to quantify energetic dysfunction in the MS brain.

<h3 style="color:#0076df">Doctoral</h3>

**Simultaneous cerebellar fNIRS and fMRI recordings**. This study pioneers the simultaneous measurement of fNIRS and fMRI signals with a standard grid montage in the cerebellum and motor cortex during a finger-tapping task. We demonstrated strong spatial and temporal correlations between these modalities and identified clear task-evoked responses. This work highlights the importance of data processing pipelines in multimodal neuroimaging, and introduces a unique pipeline that leverages data from both fNIRS and fMRI to improve signal analysis. We observed significant differences in cerebellum analysis when using different GLM pipelines.

[publication](https://zenodo.org/records/14001135) (Journal publication in progress)

**Cerebellar fNIRS image reconstruction**. We conducted a study to assess the feasibility of personalized, high-density fNIRS for cerebellar imaging and its comparability to fMRI data, sequentially acquired during a motor and a passive auditory task. Using subject-specific montages, we demonstrated fNIRS ability to detect cerebellar activation and achieve image reconstruction by using the Mininum Norm Estimation and the Maximum Entropy on the Mean algorithms. While spatial and temporal correlations between fNIRS and fMRI were moderate, we observed overlap in activation areas: an analysis of localization errors confirmed a slight displacement between fNIRS and fMRI activation clusters, consistent with prior research, likely due to anatomical constraints. We also noted significant inter-subject variability, highlighting the need for future studies with standardized montages and group-level analysis.

[publication](https://zenodo.org/records/8313255) (Journal publication in progress)

**Cerebellar fNIRS: a feasibility study**. We conducted a feasibility study on cerebellar fNIRS, addressing key challenges in signal acquisition and analysis. We successfully demonstrated the ability to measure task-evoked cerebellar responses using fNIRS, comparing results with fMRI during finger-tapping tasks. This work emphasizes the critical role of managing physiological confounds and ensuring sufficient sample size. Importantly, we provided practical guidelines for achieving accurate cerebellar fNIRS measurements and analysis, contributing to the advancement of this neuroimaging technique.

[publication](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9629565)

**Single-Trial Detection of EEG Event-Related Potentials with Integral Shape Averaging**. We developed Integral Shape Averaging (ISA) and its derivative ISAD (ISA on Delayed signals), innovative methods to overcome the limitations of traditional ERP averaging in EEG analysis. Traditional methods require strict time-locking and a large number of trials, which are often impractical for cognitive ERPs like the N400. ISAD can extract ERPs even from single trials, and both methods are robust to latency variations. This work demonstrates ISAD effectiveness in N400 extraction in children during a semantic priming test and explores its potential for studying ERP variability. The proposed algorithm has significant clinical relevance, offering a more efficient and flexible tool for analyzing evoked responses in neurological settings.

[publication](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9630271)

<h3 style="color:#0076df">Pre-doctoral</h3>

**Exploration of the physiological response to an online gambling task**. We conducted a study examining physiological arousal during online slot machine gambling, comparing a novel Electro-dermal Activity (EDA) frequency feature with traditional Heart Rate Variability (HRV) spectral parameters. We recorded EDA and ECG data from 46 participants during a 30-minute session and analyzed arousal changes across three 10-minute phases. Results showed significant differences in all parameters between the initial phase and the subsequent phases, with EDA exhibiting a distinct trend. This research highlights the potential of EDA frequency-domain analysis as a reliable and easy-to-implement method for assessing sympathetic activity, offering valuable insights for clinical applications and the study of gambling-related disorders.

[publication](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9175972)

**Heart Rate Variability from Wearables**. This study assessed the accuracy of HRV measurements from a commercial smart shirt with textile electrodes and a Photoplethysmogram (PPG) from a wristband by comparing them to standard Electrocardiographam (ECG). We evaluated HRV and Inter-Beat Intervals (IBI) data across different postures (sitting, standing, and controlled breathing). Results indicated that the ECG shirt provided more reliable HRV measurements, while the wristband's IBI showed significant differences, especially during postural changes. This research emphasizes the importance of careful interpretation of PPG-derived IBI and suggests that online HRV from ECG shirts offers a more accurate and reliable alternative. Additionally, we highlighted the need for improved wristband sensor design to minimize missing beat measurements.

[publication](https://ebooks.iospress.nl/publication/51523)

**Therapeutic services based on Wearable virtual reality and Bio-sensors (TWB)**. TWB addresses the therapeutic needs of persons with neuro-functional impairments (NFI), providing innovative services bringing wearable virtual reality (VR) apps & bio-sensing devices into a customisable platform for patient care, cure and data analysis. I participated in the initial stage of the data collection and analysis (HRV and EEG), by defining with the interdisciplinary team the necessary data types and conversion and the physiological indices that could be derived during a therapeutic session.

<h3 style="color:#0076df">Master thesis</h3>

**Design, implementation, and pilot testing of a language-independent speech intelligibility test**. we designed a language-independent speech-in-noise test for domestic hearing screening, addressing the growing incidence of hearing loss with ageing. This speech-in-noise test uses Vowel-Consonant=Vowel (VCV) words, features self-calibration, and dynamically adjusts noise levels based on environmental monitoring. Extensive simulations were conducted to characterize the VCV dataset and optimize the test parameters, including a comparative inter-language analysis of intelligibility prediction measures (STOI). A pilot study with normal hearing subjects, including non-native English speakers, showed homogeneous and reliable speech reception thresholds, indicating strong feasibility and test-retest reliability. The results suggest this test has the potential to become a valuable tool for early hearing-impairment detection in multilingual settings.

[publication1](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/9/5344) [publication2](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2020_AJA-19-00071)

<h3 style="color:#0076df">Bachelor thesis</h3>

**Validation and optimisation of an automatic hard-tissues segmentation algorithm for CBCT scan**. We conducted a validation study of an automatic algorithm for hard tissue segmentation in CBCT data. The algorithm sensitivity was rigorously evaluated, focusing on structures highly susceptible to threshold variations. Accuracy was assessed through both quantitative and qualitative comparisons of manual and automatic segmentations. Results demonstrated the algorithm robustness, with low sensitivity and high accuracy, and no statistically significant differences compared to manual segmentations. Furthermore, we developed and proposed two optimized versions of the algorithm to reduce computational time and eliminate user dependence without compromising accuracy.

[publication](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281178089_Validation_of_an_Automatic_Hard_Tissue_Segmentation_Algorithm_for_Cone_Beam_CT_Data)
